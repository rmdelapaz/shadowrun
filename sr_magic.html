Magic

How magic works in Shadowrun Fifth Edition: traditions, spellcasting, Drain, the astral plane, spirits and adepts.

The Return of Wonder

On December 24, 2011, the world changed forever. Magic - real, undeniable, reality-altering magic - returned to Earth after millennia of absence. This event, known as the "Awakening," didn't just add fantasy elements to a cyberpunk world; it fundamentally transformed human understanding of reality itself.

Imagine waking up one morning to discover that physics has new rules, that ancient myths are walking the streets, and that some people can literally reshape reality with their thoughts. This isn't Harry Potter with smartphones - this is magic that follows scientific principles, operates within established laws, and integrates seamlessly with cutting-edge technology.

Understanding Mana: The Fuel of Magic

🔮 How Mana Works

Mana is the fundamental force that powers all magic in Shadowrun. Think of it as the "electricity" of the magical world - a form of energy that flows through everything but can only be manipulated by those with the right "wiring" (magical ability).

Key Principles:

  • Mana is everywhere: It permeates all living things and many objects
  • Varying density: Some areas have more mana than others
  • Manipulation requires talent: Only Awakened individuals can consciously use it
  • Technology interaction: Mana and high technology don't always play nice

Background Count

Places soaked in strong emotion or damage to the living world carry a background count that makes magic harder to work there:

  • Tainted places: Sites of violence, pain or massacre, toxic and polluted zones
  • Aspected places: Sites tuned to one tradition or purpose, which help magic of that kind and hinder the rest
  • In play: Background count costs Awakened characters dice, so a battlefield or a toxic dump is a bad place to fight a mage on fair terms

Magical Traditions: Paths to Power

Magic isn't a single unified force - it's understood and channeled through different philosophical and cultural frameworks called "traditions." Your tradition shapes how you view magic, what spirits you can summon, and how you cast spells:

🔬

Hermetic Tradition

The Scientific Approach: Magic as applied metaphysics, studied like any other science through rigorous research and experimentation.

Core Philosophy

Hermetic mages view magic as a natural force that can be understood, catalogued, and systematically applied. They use ritual circles, precise formulae, and extensive study to achieve their effects.

Characteristics

  • Spirits: Elementals (Fire, Earth, Air, Water) and spirits of man
  • Drain resisted with: Willpower + Logic
  • Focus: Intellectual understanding and precise control
  • Tools: Spell formulae, ritual circles, laboratory equipment
  • Advancement: Through study, experimentation, and academic progression

Real-World Analogy

Think PhD researcher meets wizard - they approach magic like nuclear physics, with careful theories, controlled experiments, and peer review.

🐺

Shamanic Tradition

The Spiritual Connection: Magic as communion with the spirit world, accessed through personal relationships and natural harmony.

Core Philosophy

Shamanic practitioners see magic as part of the natural world's spiritual ecosystem. They work with spirit allies, respect natural cycles, and view themselves as mediators between the physical and spirit realms.

Characteristics

  • Spirits: Beast spirits, nature spirits (Earth, Air, Water) and spirits of man
  • Drain resisted with: Willpower + Charisma
  • Focus: Intuitive understanding and spiritual relationship
  • Tools: Mentor spirits (the old "totems"), sacred objects, natural materials
  • Advancement: Through vision quests, spiritual growth, mentor guidance

Modern Adaptation

Urban shamans find spirits in city ecosystems - pigeon spirits, concrete elementals, neon totems. They adapt ancient wisdom to modern environments.

⚡

Chaos Magic

The Paradigm Hacker (an optional tradition from Street Grimoire): Magic as reality programming, where belief shapes reality and contradictions create power.

Core Philosophy

Chaos mages reject fixed traditions, instead believing that all magical systems are equally valid tools. They adopt different paradigms as needed, mixing and matching techniques from various sources.

Characteristics

  • Spirits: A fixed list like any tradition, dressed in whatever paradigm the mage favors
  • Focus: Adaptability and paradigm shifting
  • Tools: Anything that reinforces current belief system
  • Advancement: Through paradigm breaks and reality hacking

Practical Application

A chaos mage might dress a fireball in hermetic formulae in the morning and summon with a shamanic chant at noon. At the table it uses the same spellcasting and summoning rules as everyone else: the difference is flavor, the spirit list and the Drain attribute.

☯️

Wuxing Tradition

The Balanced Path (another Street Grimoire tradition): Magic as harmony between opposing forces, following ancient Eastern principles of balance and flow.

Core Philosophy

Wuxing practitioners understand magic through the interaction of five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) and the balance of yin and yang. Magic flows naturally when these forces are in harmony.

Characteristics

  • Spirits: Drawn from the five-element cycle
  • Focus: Balance, harmony, and natural flow
  • Tools: I Ching, feng shui principles, elemental focuses
  • Advancement: Through meditation, balance, and understanding of cycles

Corporate Integration

Many Wuxing mages work for corporations, applying magical principles to business strategy, architecture, and even stock market analysis.

Combat Magic

Purpose: Inflicting damage and controlling battlefield conditions

Examples: Manabolt (pure magical damage), Fireball (elemental area effect), Stunbolt (non-lethal force)

Drain: Generally high - magic demands a price for violence

Tactics: Often the last resort for professional mages

Detection Magic

Purpose: Gathering information and enhancing perception

Examples: Detect Life (sensing living beings), Mind Probe (reading thoughts), Clairvoyance (seeing a distant place)

Drain: Usually low - the universe likes to share information

Tactics: Essential for reconnaissance and investigation

Health Magic

Purpose: Healing injuries and enhancing physical capabilities

Examples: Heal (repairing damage), Increase Reflexes (enhanced speed), Antidote (fighting toxins)

Drain: Moderate - healing is natural but taxing

Tactics: Support magic that keeps the team operational

Illusion Magic

Purpose: Deceiving senses and altering perceptions

Examples: Invisibility (hiding from sight), Physical Mask (changing appearance), Confusion (mental disorientation)

Drain: Low to moderate - illusion flows easily

Tactics: Perfect for infiltration and misdirection

Manipulation Magic

Purpose: Controlling physical objects and mental states

Examples: Levitate (moving objects), Control Thoughts (mental domination), Poltergeist (chaotic telekinesis)

Drain: High - forcing change against nature's will

Tactics: Powerful but expensive, use sparingly

Drain: The Price of Power

Magic isn't free. Every spell exacts a cost called "Drain" - the physical and mental exhaustion that comes from channeling supernatural forces through a mortal mind and body:

Drain Resistance, Step by Step

> Hermetic mage, Magic 6, Willpower 5, Logic 5

> Casting a combat spell at Force 5

> Drain Value comes from the SPELL, not from the Force alone

> This spell reads F-3, so at Force 5 the Drain Value is 2

> Resisting with Willpower + Logic = 10 dice ... 4 hits

> 4 hits vs Drain Value 2 -> no damage taken

Mage condition: fine

> Now overcasting the same spell at Force 8 (Magic is only 6)

> Drain Value 5, and casting this far above your Magic can turn the Drain PHYSICAL

> Willpower + Logic ... only 2 hits

> 3 boxes of damage. If they land as PHYSICAL, that is a hospital trip, not a headache.

Three common Drain mistakes

Drain Value is not Force ÷ 2. Every spell carries its own Drain code written relative to Force — F−3, F−1, F+2 and so on — so two spells cast at the same Force can hurt you very differently. Look the code up on the spell, then apply the Force. (There is also a floor: a Drain Value never drops below 2.)

You do not resist Drain with Magic. You resist it with Willpower + a mental attribute set by your tradition — Logic for hermetics, Charisma for shamans. Each hit soaks one box. This is why a high-Magic mage with poor Willpower is a liability: enormous spells, no capacity to pay for them.

Drain is usually Stun damage, but not always. High-Force casting can make the Drain Physical; check the core rulebook for the exact trigger your table uses. That is the guardrail on the whole magic system: you may choose any Force up to twice your Magic, and nothing stops you reaching high except what it does to your body on the way back.

Understanding Drain

  • The casting test: Spellcasting + Magic [Force]. You choose the Force each time, up to twice your Magic, and hits above the Force don't count
  • Stun Drain: The normal case — mental exhaustion, headaches, nosebleeds
  • Physical Drain: The risk of high-Force casting, which can turn Drain into real wounds
  • Resistance: Willpower + your tradition’s mental attribute (Logic for hermetics, Charisma for shamans) — never Magic
  • Consequences: Accumulates and can knock out or kill careless mages

What You See in Astral Space

  • Living Auras: Every living being glows with their emotional and magical state
  • Magical Effects: Spells, wards, and enchantments appear as visible constructs
  • Spirits: Native astral beings that exist naturally in this realm
  • Emotional Imprints: Psychic residue from strong emotions or traumatic events
  • Mana Lines: Flows of magical energy connecting power sites

Astral Projection vs Astral Perception

  • Astral Perception: Seeing both physical and astral simultaneously - like magical "x-ray vision"
  • Astral Projection: Leaving your physical body behind to travel in pure spirit form
  • Risks: Projected mages are vulnerable to astral threats and their body lies defenseless
  • Benefits: Astral travel ignores physical barriers and distance

Spirit Summoning: Calling Otherworldly Allies

One of magic's most powerful applications is summoning spirits - intelligent entities from the astral plane who can provide services, information, and supernatural assistance:

The Summoning Process

  1. Choose: Pick one of the spirit types your tradition can call, and how powerful (its Force)
  2. Summon: Roll against the spirit; if you win, it owes you a number of services
  3. Pay the Drain: Summoning a strong spirit hurts, just like casting a strong spell
  4. Services: The spirit performs tasks until its services run out (an unbound spirit does not hang around forever)
  5. Binding (Optional): A longer, costlier ritual that keeps a spirit on call - spirits aren't slaves, and they remember how they were treated
  6. Release: Let the spirit go when the job is done

Spirit Types and Services

Each tradition has its own list of spirit types; in practice they fill jobs like these:

  • Combat Spirits: Magical muscle for dangerous situations
  • Detection Spirits: Scouts and information gatherers
  • Manipulation Spirits: Telekinetic assistants
  • Guardian Spirits: Protectors and watchers
  • Task Spirits: Specialists for specific jobs

Adept Philosophy

Physical adepts follow various martial and philosophical traditions that shape how they develop their powers:

  • Way of the Warrior: Combat-focused development
  • Way of the Invisible: Stealth and infiltration powers
  • Way of the Artisan: Creative and craft enhancement
  • Way of the Athlete: Physical perfection and competition

Magical Security and Countermeasures

In 2075, corporate security includes magical defenses just as sophisticated as their electronic and physical systems:

PROTECTED
FACILITY

Magical Security Systems

  • Wards: Ritual barriers that block astral projection and enemy magic
  • Watchers: Simple spirits that patrol and report intrusions
  • Bound Spirits: Powerful entities providing active defense
  • Mage Security: Human magic users on staff
  • Magical Alarms: Spells that trigger on magical intrusion
  • Astral Barriers: Walls that exist only in the spirit world

Countering Magical Security

  • Dispelling: Destroying enemy magic with targeted spells
  • Spirit Negotiation: Convincing guard spirits to look the other way
  • Masking: Hiding magical signatures and auras
  • Astral Combat: Fighting security spirits on their home turf
  • Ward Breaching: Creating temporary gaps in magical barriers

Enchanting and Magical Items

Magic can be stored, programmed, and built into physical objects, creating a whole industry of magical crafting and enhancement:

Magical Item Creation

Enhances specific spells

Enchanting Process

  1. Design Phase: Plan the item's magical properties
  2. Material Preparation: Gather appropriate magical reagents
  3. Ritual Creation: Perform extended magical working
  4. Binding: Link the enchantment to the physical object
  5. Testing: Verify the item works as intended

Types of Magical Items

  • Foci: Items that enhance magical abilities
  • Reagents: Magical materials consumed by rituals and alchemy
  • Qi Foci: Items that hold an adept power
  • Alchemical Preparations: Objects that hold a spell until it is triggered

Practical Implications

  • Cyberware Penalty: Every implant lowers Essence, and lost Essence drags Magic down with it
  • Object Resistance: The more processed and high-tech an object is, the harder it is to affect with a spell
  • Magical Devices: Technology designed to work with magic
  • Hybrid Systems: Combinations of magical and technological effects

Advanced Magical Concepts

Metamagic: Magic About Magic

As mages advance, they learn to manipulate the fundamental nature of magic itself through metamagic techniques:

graph TD A[Awakened character] --> B["Initiation: 10 + grade × 3 Karma"] B --> C{Learn a metamagic} C --> D[Centering] C --> E[Shielding] C --> F[Masking] C --> G[Quickening] D --> H[Initiate again for the next grade] E --> H F --> H G --> H

Metamagic Examples

  • Centering: A focusing discipline (a chant, a kata, a mantra) that steadies the magician under pressure
  • Shielding: Better defense against hostile spells
  • Masking: Disguising your aura so you read as mundane, or as something else
  • Quickening: Paying Karma to make a sustained spell last without being sustained

Playing Awakened Characters

Playing a magical character in Shadowrun requires understanding both the mechanical systems and the narrative implications of wielding supernatural power:

Mage Considerations

  • Versatility vs Specialization: Broad spell knowledge or deep expertise?
  • Drain Management: Balancing power with personal safety
  • Spirit Relationships: Building alliances in the astral realm
  • Magical Security: You're vulnerable to astral threats
  • Social Implications: How does society view your abilities?

Adept Considerations

  • Power Focus: Combat, stealth, social, or utility abilities?
  • Martial Training: Which fighting style shapes your development?
  • Magic Rating: An adept gets power points equal to Magic, so every point of Essence lost to cyberware costs powers too
  • Mundane Integration: How do you hide superhuman abilities?

Your Magical Journey Ahead

Magic in Shadowrun isn't just a game mechanic - it's a fundamental force that shapes society, politics, economics, and personal identity. Whether you're a corporate wage-mage casting marketing spells, a street shaman protecting your community, or a shadowrunning spellslinger using fireballs to make ends meet, magic opens up endless possibilities for character development and storytelling.

The next part of this guide leaves the astral for the other invisible world runners work in: the Matrix, where deckers and technomancers do the same kind of job with code and Resonance instead of mana.

Remember: With great power comes great drain resistance. Magic is a tool, but wisdom is knowing when not to use it.
