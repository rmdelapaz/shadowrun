The Matrix

How the wireless Matrix of 2075 works: personas, the four Matrix attributes, marks, Overwatch, IC, and technomancers.

Welcome to the Matrix

In the Sixth World, the internet isn't just a network - it's a parallel universe. The Matrix is a globally distributed virtual reality where information takes physical form, programs appear as people or creatures, and digital warfare is fought with the intensity of physical combat.

Imagine if the entire internet was rendered as a massive, immersive 3D environment that you could navigate as if it were a real place. Now imagine that environment is inhabited by AI security programs that can literally kill you through your neural interface. That's the Matrix - where Tron meets Neuromancer meets The Matrix, but with the very real possibility of flatlining.

Since Crash 2.0 in 2064 the Matrix has been wireless: nearly every device, gun and door lock is online, and most people experience the Matrix through augmented reality (AR) overlays on what they already see. Deckers and technomancers can go further and drop into full virtual reality (VR).

Matrix Architecture: The Digital Ecosystem

The Matrix isn't chaotic - it has structure, geography, and rules. Understanding this architecture is crucial for navigation and survival:

Matrix Layers and Regions

flowchart TD A[Public Grid] --> B[Corporate Hosts] A --> C[Government Systems] A --> D[Personal Area Networks] A -.-> J[Data Havens] A -.-> M[Resonance Wells] B --> E[Secure Subnets] C --> F[Classified Networks] D --> G[Your Own Devices] E --> H[Black IC Zones] F --> I[Military Networks]

Key Matrix Regions

  • Public Grid: The "surface web" - accessible to everyone, lightly secured
  • Corporate Hosts: Private company networks with serious security
  • Government Systems: Official databases with military-grade protection
  • Data Havens: Neutral zones where information brokers operate
  • Personal Area Networks (PANs): A runner's own devices, slaved to their commlink or deck
  • Resonance Wells: Areas where technomancers draw power

Digital Personas: Who You Are in Cyberspace

In the Matrix, everyone appears as a digital avatar called a "persona." Your persona is more than just appearance - it reflects your capabilities, intentions, and sometimes your very soul:

Decker Persona

The Digital Warrior: Crafted for functionality and intimidation. Often resembles futuristic soldiers, cybernetic ninjas, or iconic hacker archetypes.

Characteristics:

  • Highly customizable appearance
  • Tool-focused design elements
  • Combat-ready accessories
  • Status displays and HUDs

Common Forms:

  • Cyber-samurai with data-katanas
  • Tactical operator with digital weapons
  • Abstract geometric constructs
  • Classical hackers with terminal interfaces

Technomancer Persona

The Living Code: Organic, flowing, often incorporating elements that seem to breathe and grow. These personas reflect their users' Resonance.

Characteristics:

  • Organic, flowing designs
  • Constantly shifting elements
  • Bio-digital integration
  • Resonance energy displays

Common Forms:

  • Bio-mechanical angels
  • Evolving digital organisms
  • Energy beings with living auras
  • Shamanic totems with tech integration

Spider Persona

The Digital Sentinel: Corporate security specialists with personas designed for system defense and counterintrusion operations.

Characteristics:

  • Authoritative, intimidating design
  • Corporate branding elements
  • Multi-armed or multi-eyed features
  • Defensive posturing

Common Forms:

  • Multi-armed corporate enforcers
  • Digital spiders with company logos
  • Armored security officers
  • Abstract corporate symbols

IC Persona

The Autonomous Hunter: Intrusion Countermeasures appear as various threatening entities designed to identify, track, and eliminate unauthorized users.

Characteristics:

  • Threatening, non-human appearance
  • Functionality-based design
  • Predatory behavior patterns
  • System integration elements

Common Forms:

  • Digital bloodhounds for tracking
  • Armored warriors for combat
  • Viral clouds for system damage
  • Abstract geometric threats

Intrusion Countermeasures (IC): The Digital Immune System

Every host can launch IC (Intrusion Countermeasures) - autonomous programs that find and punish intruders. IC strength is tied to the host's rating, and a host usually launches it once it notices you, for example after a failed Sleaze action. Think of IC as the Matrix's immune system. A few of the common kinds:

Patrol IC

Watchdog programs that look for intruders and illegal actions inside the host. They're the digital equivalent of security cameras with brains.

Tar Baby IC

Sticky programs that link-lock intruders so they can't jack out, like digital quicksand that holds you while the rest of security arrives.

Killer IC

Combat programs that hit your persona with straight Matrix damage, wrecking your deck rather than your brain.

Black IC

Lethal programs that link-lock you and send biofeedback into your brain along with the Matrix damage. In hot-sim that damage is Physical. These are the digital death penalty.

Track IC

Hunter programs that follow your signal back to its source and report your physical location to the host's owners.

Blaster IC

Programs that link-lock you and hit you with biofeedback, aimed at the person behind the persona.

What a Cyberdeck Gives You

  • Device Rating: The deck's overall quality
  • Attribute array: Four numbers you assign to Attack, Sleaze, Data Processing and Firewall, and can swap around as the run changes. A plain commlink has only Data Processing and Firewall, which is why it can't hack
  • Program slots: How many programs (Hammer, Armor, Stealth, Biofeedback Filter and so on) you can run at once
  • Hot-sim capable: Decks let you run VR at full speed, with all the risk that brings

Technomancers: The Matrix Mages

Living in the Code

Technomancers represent one of the most revolutionary concepts in Shadowrun - individuals who can interface with the Matrix using only their minds, no cyberdeck required. They're not just using the Matrix; they are part of it.

The Resonance

Where deckers use technology and mages use magic, technomancers tap into the "Resonance" - a force that binds all digital information together. Think of it as the Matrix's equivalent of mana, but instead of manipulating physical reality, it manipulates digital reality.

Complex Forms vs Programs

  • Programs: Static code written by programmers and run on a deck
  • Complex Forms: Effects a technomancer weaves from Resonance by pure thought (4 Karma each to learn)
  • Fading: The price of using a complex form, resisted with Resonance + Willpower, much as a mage resists Drain
  • Sprites: Digital entities that technomancers compile and can register for longer service
  • Living persona: A technomancer's own mind serves as their deck; its Device Rating equals their Resonance and its four Matrix attributes come from their mental attributes

Technomancer Abilities

mindmap root((Technomancer)) [Complex Forms] Attack Stealth Analysis Control [Sprites] Courier Crack Data Fault Machine [Resonance] Living Persona Submersion Echoes

The Double-Edged Sword

Technomancers are incredibly powerful but also incredibly vulnerable. Their direct neural connection to the Matrix means:

  • No hardware dependencies: Can't be disarmed of their "deck"
  • No deck to upgrade: They grow by raising Resonance and learning complex forms, not by shopping
  • Sprite assistance: Can call digital spirits for help
  • But also: In VR a technomancer is always in hot-sim, so biofeedback hits them as Physical damage
  • Corporate interest: Megacorps want to study and control them

Marks and Overwatch: The Two Rules That Actually Govern a Run

Everything above describes what the Matrix is. These two systems decide what happens to you in it, and a decker who does not understand both will get the team caught.

Marks: the currency of access

You do not "hack into" a system in Fifth Edition. You place marks on it — a mark is a token of access on a specific icon, and almost every worthwhile Matrix action requires you to already hold one, two, or three marks on the target.

Getting a markUsesTrade-off
Hack on the Fly Your Sleaze Quiet. If it fails, the target places a mark on you and knows you're there; a host will launch IC.
Brute Force Your Attack Loud. The target knows it was attacked, and if it fails you take Matrix damage.

Both are illegal actions, so either one raises your Overwatch Score whether it works or not. You can hold a maximum of three marks on any one icon. You may go for more than one in a single attempt, but it costs you: −4 dice to grab two at once, −10 to grab three.

Why this matters at the table

The choice between Hack on the Fly and Brute Force is the decker's entire personality. Sleaze is slow and survivable; Attack is fast and expensive. Deckers who only ever Brute Force get the job done and get the team converged on — which brings us to the clock.

Overwatch Score: the clock you cannot turn off

The Grid Overwatch Division — GOD — is the Matrix's police force, and it is always looking. Every decker carries an Overwatch Score that only ever goes up during a run:

  • Illegal actions — anything using your Attack or Sleaze attributes — add to it: +1 for every hit your target rolls to defend. Legal actions using Data Processing or Firewall do not.
  • It climbs on its own regardless: 2d6 every 15 minutes, rolled secretly by the GM. Loitering is not free.
  • Nothing you do reduces it. The only reset is jacking out completely — and then you start the next intrusion from scratch.

When the score hits the convergence threshold (40 in the core rules), GOD converges: your persona takes 12 boxes of Matrix damage, your device is forced to reboot (with dumpshock if you were in VR), and — the part that ends runs — your physical location is traced and handed to the authorities.

This is the Matrix's real timer

New deckers plan around IC. Experienced deckers plan around Overwatch. IC is a fight you can win; convergence is a clock you cannot stop, only outrun. It is why the correct answer to "can I take twenty minutes to look around the host?" is almost always no, and why a good decker jacks out and back in between objectives rather than staying resident.

It also quietly answers a question new players always ask — why doesn't the decker just do everything from home, a week in advance? Because the clock starts the moment they touch anything illegal, and it does not care how clever the plan was.

> 4 net hits: Matrix damage to the deck, biofeedback to the decker
> Hot-sim: that biofeedback is Physical damage. Link-locked: no jacking out!

Matrix Combat Principles

  • Initiative: depends entirely on how you are connected — see the table above. In VR it is Data Processing + Intuition, not Reaction
  • Attack: the Data Spike action is how you do damage; the Hammer program makes it hurt more
  • Defense: you resist Matrix damage with your device's rating + Firewall; the Armor program helps
  • Damage Types: Matrix damage hits your device; biofeedback hits you, as Stun in cold-sim and Physical in hot-sim
  • Link-lock: IC can prevent emergency disconnection

Professional hacking isn't about randomly trying passwords - it's a systematic approach to understanding, penetrating, and controlling target systems:

Core Hacking Actions

💡 Professional Hacking Tips

  • Matrix Perception: Always scan before you hack - knowledge saves lives
  • Hack on the Fly: Quiet way to place a mark, using your Sleaze
  • Brute Force: Loud way to place a mark, using your Attack; the target knows
  • Sleaze actions: Everything quiet runs off Sleaze, and a failure gets you noticed
  • Data Spike: Combat action for destroying IC
  • Jack Out: Disconnecting when things go wrong; you can't while link-locked

The Four Matrix Attributes (ASDF)

Decks and hosts have all four; a commlink has only Data Processing and Firewall. Each one is also the limit on the actions that use it.

  • Attack: Your ability to damage IC and crack encryption
  • Sleaze: Your stealth rating for avoiding detection
  • Data Processing: Your speed and multitasking ability
  • Firewall: Your defense against IC attacks and traces

Data: The Ultimate Currency

In the corporate wars of 2075, information is more valuable than gold, diamonds, or military hardware. Understanding what data to target can make the difference between a milk run and a retirement score:

Types of Valuable Data

  • Paydata: Information that clients specifically want to buy
  • Corporate Secrets: Research data, financial records, strategic plans
  • Personal Information: Blackmail material, private communications
  • Access Codes: Passwords, biometric data, security protocols
  • Financial Data: Account numbers, transaction records, audit trails
  • Technical Specifications: Product designs, manufacturing processes

Data Security Measures

  • Encryption: Mathematical locks requiring specific keys
  • Data Bombs: Destructive programs that trigger on unauthorized access
  • Host IC: Valuable files sit inside hosts, behind IC and spiders
  • Biometric Locks: Security requiring specific user identity
  • Temporal Locks: Data that self-destructs after time limits

Matrix Threats Beyond IC

The Matrix isn't just defended by programs - it's populated by hostile entities that make corporate security look friendly:

AI Entities

  • Wild sprites: Resonance beings with agendas of their own (note: “otaku” were the Third Edition version of the Matrix-native human; after Crash 2.0 they gave way to technomancers)
  • Dissonance Entities: Corrupted, hostile sprites and the dissonant technomancers who work with them
  • Rogue AIs: True artificial intelligences pursuing their own agendas
  • Corporate Spiders: Human security specialists defending their turf

Environmental Hazards

  • Resonance Storms: Chaotic areas that damage personas
  • Data Havens in Flux: Unstable regions with shifting reality
  • Viral Zones: Areas infected with self-replicating malware
  • Dead Zones: Matrix regions with no connectivity

Matrix Etiquette and Shadow Community

The Matrix has its own social rules and underground communities. Understanding these can mean the difference between finding allies and making enemies:

Hacker Culture

  • Handle Respect: Your Matrix name is your reputation
  • Information Sharing: Trading intel builds relationships
  • Corp Hatred: United opposition to corporate control
  • Artistic Expression: Elegant hacks are valued as art
  • No Snitching: Never reveal another hacker's identity

Underground Resources

  • Hacker BBSs: Hidden forums for information exchange
  • Pirate Data Havens: Neutral territories for data storage
  • Shadow Markets: Black markets for programs and data
  • Sandboxes: Safe spaces to practice hacking skills
