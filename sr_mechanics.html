Game Mechanics

How Shadowrun Fifth Edition rolls dice, counts hits, caps them with limits, and builds a runner with the Priority system.

The Heart of Shadowrun: Dice Pools

Shadowrun uses a dice system that is both elegant and gritty. Instead of rolling a single die and adding modifiers, you build a "dice pool" - a handful of six-sided dice equal to a skill plus its linked attribute, plus or minus any modifiers. Bonuses and penalties add or remove dice; the number you need on each die never changes. Think of it like assembling a team for a specific task: the more qualified people you bring, the better your chances of success.

Interactive Dice Pool Example

Scenario: Your street samurai is shooting at a corporate security guard: Pistols + Agility, plus 2 dice from a wireless smartgun, with the gun's Accuracy as the limit.

If the pistol's Accuracy is 5, any hits past 5 don't count (see Limits below), and the chance dips only slightly, to 81.0%.

Success and Failure: The Hit System

In Shadowrun, each die that shows 5 or 6 is a "hit." You need a certain number of hits to succeed at different tasks:

Difficulty Thresholds

The GM sets a threshold, the number of hits you need. If no threshold is given, one hit is enough. A test is written as the pool, the limit in brackets and the threshold in parentheses: Hardware + Logic [Mental] (2).

  • Easy (1): picking a basic lock, driving in normal traffic
  • Average (2): smooth-talking a bored guard, patching up a commlink
  • Hard (4): disarming a bomb against the clock
  • Very Hard (6): performing surgery under fire
  • Extreme (8–10): the kind of feat people tell stories about

For a sense of scale: 6 dice score at least 1 hit 91.2% of the time and at least 2 hits 64.9% of the time; 12 dice reach a Hard threshold of 4 hits 60.7% of the time.

Other Kinds of Test

  • Opposed tests: both sides roll and the one with more hits wins. The winner's extra hits are net hits. Ties go to the defender; in combat, a tie is a grazing hit that does no damage.
  • Extended tests: you roll repeatedly and add up hits toward a large threshold, losing 1 die from your pool on each roll after the first.
  • Defaulting: with no rating in a skill you roll the linked attribute − 1 (some skills can't be defaulted at all).
  • Buying hits: with the GM's approval you can skip the roll and take 1 hit for every 4 dice in your pool, rounded down. It is all or nothing, and not for tests where a glitch would matter.

The Beauty of Extra Hits

Unlike many RPGs where you either succeed or fail, Shadowrun rewards excellence. Extra hits beyond the threshold represent degrees of success:

  • Shooting: Extra hits increase damage
  • Hacking: Extra hits provide additional access or stealth
  • Social: Extra hits give more information or better deals
  • Magic: Extra hits increase spell effectiveness

Limits: The Ceiling on Your Hits

Every skill test has a limit, the most hits that can count. Roll 9 hits against a limit of 6 and you get 6. Each character has three inherent limits, each rounded up:

  • Physical limit: [(Strength × 2) + Body + Reaction] ÷ 3
  • Mental limit: [(Logic × 2) + Intuition + Willpower] ÷ 3
  • Social limit: [(Charisma × 2) + Willpower + Essence] ÷ 3

Example: a runner with Strength 4, Body 5 and Reaction 5 has a Physical limit of (8 + 5 + 5) ÷ 3 = 6. With Logic 3, Intuition 4 and Willpower 3, her Mental limit is 13 ÷ 3 = 4.33, rounded up to 5.

Gear often supplies the limit instead. A gun's Accuracy, a cyberdeck's attributes and a spell's Force replace your inherent limit for that test, whether they are higher or lower. Tests that use only attributes (no skill) have no limit at all.

Glitches: When the Dice Turn on You

Count the 1s as well as the hits. If more than half of the dice in your pool show 1, you have glitched: something goes wrong, even if you still score enough hits to succeed. A glitch with zero hits is a critical glitch, and that is a disaster.

Big pools rarely glitch. A single die glitches (and critically glitches) one time in six; 2 dice glitch 2.8% of the time; 6 dice only 0.9%; 13 dice only 0.2%.

"More than half", not "half or more"

The Fifth Edition core rulebook says more than half the dice. "Half or more" is the Fourth Edition wording, and it also appears in the free SR5 Quick-Start Rules. This guide uses the core rule: with 6 dice, three 1s is not a glitch; four 1s is.

Edge: Your Luck Stat

Edge is a special attribute that measures luck. Humans start with Edge 2 (maximum 7); elves, dwarves, orks and trolls start with 1 (maximum 6). You can spend at most one point of Edge on any one test, and only on your own actions:

  • Push the Limit: add your Edge rating in dice and ignore the limit. Spend it before the roll and every 6 in the pool explodes (roll it again and keep counting); spend it after the roll and only the added Edge dice explode.
  • Second Chance: reroll all the dice that did not score hits. It can't undo a glitch.
  • Seize the Initiative: act first in every Initiative Pass of the Combat Turn.
  • Blitz: roll the maximum 5 Initiative Dice for one Combat Turn.
  • Close Call: negate a glitch, or turn a critical glitch into an ordinary glitch.
  • Dead Man's Trigger: when you are about to fall unconscious or die, make one last action.

You can also burn a point of Edge permanently: Smackdown gives an automatic success with 4 net hits, and Not Dead Yet lets you survive something that should have killed you. Spent Edge comes back slowly: 1 point after a good meal and 8 hours' sleep, plus whatever your GM awards for great play. Your GM decides when Edge refreshes beyond that.

How much it helps: 8 dice with a limit of 4 reach a threshold of 4 only 25.9% of the time. Push the Limit before the roll with Edge 3 (11 exploding dice, no limit) raises that to 64.0%; Second Chance raises it to 75.0%.

Initiative and Action

At the start of each Combat Turn everyone rolls Initiative: Reaction + Intuition + 1D6. Augmentations and magic add more Initiative Dice, up to a maximum of 5D6. The highest score acts first. When everyone has acted, subtract 10 from every score; anyone still above 0 acts again in the next Initiative Pass.

Example: a wired-up samurai with Reaction + Intuition 9 and 3D6 rolls 11, for a score of 20. She acts at 20 and again at 10, then drops to 0 and is done. A guard with 6 + 1D6 rolls a 4, scores 10 and acts once.

On your Action Phase you take two Simple Actions or one Complex Action, plus one Free Action. Other modes use other formulas: astral Initiative is (Intuition × 2) + 2D6, AR Matrix Initiative is Reaction + Intuition + 1D6, cold-sim VR is Data Processing + Intuition + 3D6, and hot-sim VR is Data Processing + Intuition + 4D6.

Damage and Condition Monitors

A ranged attack is weapon skill + Agility [Accuracy] opposed by the defender's Reaction + Intuition. Net hits add to the weapon's Damage Value (DV). The target then resists with Body + Armor (Armor reduced by the weapon's Armor Penetration, AP), and each hit reduces the DV by 1. If the modified DV is less than the modified Armor, the damage is Stun instead of Physical.

Example: the samurai fires a pistol with DV 8P and AP −1 and wins by 3 net hits: DV 11. The guard's armor 12 drops to 11 against AP −1. DV 11 is not less than 11, so the damage is Physical. He rolls Body 4 + Armor 11 = 15 dice, gets 5 hits and takes 11 − 5 = 6 boxes of Physical damage.

Every character has two condition monitors:

  • Physical: 8 + (Body ÷ 2, rounded up) boxes. The guard above, with Body 4, has 10, so 6 boxes leaves him on his feet but hurting.
  • Stun: 8 + (Willpower ÷ 2, rounded up) boxes. Fill it and you fall unconscious.

Every 3 boxes filled on a track gives a −1 wound modifier to your dice pools (and Initiative), and the two tracks add together. The guard's 6 boxes cost him 2 dice on everything. Stun damage beyond the Stun track overflows into the Physical track at 1 box for every 2. Physical damage past the end of the track overflows up to your Body in extra boxes; past that, the character dies.

Character Attributes: Your Natural Talents

Every character has eight core attributes that represent their basic capabilities, rated 1 to 6 for a human (metatypes shift that range). Think of these as your character's hardware specifications. On top of them sit the special attributes: Edge, Essence (starts at 6 and drops with every implant), and Magic or Resonance for the Awakened and technomancers.

Body

Physical Structure: Health, muscle mass, and physical resilience. Determines how much damage you can take and how much you can lift.

Example: A Body 6 troll can shrug off gunfire that would drop a Body 2 elf.

Agility

Physical Coordination: Hand-eye coordination, balance, and fine motor control. Used for shooting, sneaking, and acrobatics.

Example: High Agility lets you thread a needle or thread a bullet through a crowd.

Reaction

Combat Reflexes: How quickly you respond to danger. Determines initiative in combat and reaction to surprises.

Example: High Reaction means you draw first and dodge bullets like Neo.

Strength

Raw Power: Muscle strength and physical force. Used for melee damage, lifting, and breaking things.

Example: A Strength 8 troll can punch through a brick wall.

Willpower

Mental Fortitude: Determination, self-control, and resistance to mental influence. Your mental armor.

Example: High Willpower resists mind control spells and intimidation.

Logic

Analytical Thinking: Problem-solving, pattern recognition, and systematic reasoning. The foundation of hacking and technical skills.

Example: Logic helps you crack codes and diagnose technical problems.

Intuition

Gut Instincts: Situational awareness, empathy, and subconscious processing. Your "spider sense" for danger.

Example: Intuition tells you when someone's lying or when you're being watched.

Charisma

Personal Magnetism: Social presence, leadership ability, and force of personality. Your social operating system.

Example: High Charisma makes people want to help you or follow your lead.

Skills: Your Learned Abilities

The Skill System

Skills represent training and experience. When you attempt an action, you combine the relevant Attribute + Skill to form your dice pool. Think of attributes as your natural talent and skills as your training:

Skill Rating Scale

  • 0: Untrained (default to the linked attribute − 1, if the skill allows it)
  • 1-2: Novice (basic training, amateur level)
  • 3-4: Professional (career-level competence)
  • 5-6: Expert (among the best in your field)
  • 7+: Legendary (world-class skill)

It is your allowance of Special Attribute Points, and Special Attributes means exactly three things: Edge, Magic and Resonance. Nothing else.

This is the single most misread cell on the sheet. Taking troll at Priority A does not make you a better troll — trolls have the same racial modifiers whatever priority you bought them at. What the high priority buys is points to spend on Edge. And a mundane street samurai has nowhere to put those points except Edge, which is precisely why mundane characters at a high metatype priority end up so lucky.

The rule that catches every new player

You must assign A, B, C, D and E exactly once each across the five columns. You cannot take two Bs. Every point of brilliance in one column is paid for by mediocrity in another — that constraint is the character creation minigame, and it is why so many runners take Magic at E and stay mundane.

Priority Decision Examples

Street Samurai Build Example

  • A - Resources: Need expensive cyberware and weapons
  • B - Attributes: Physical stats are crucial for combat
  • C - Skills: Focus on combat and infiltration skills
  • D - Metatype: Human works fine for this role
  • E - Magic: No magical ability (mundane)

This creates a chrome-enhanced human killing machine with top-tier gear.

Mage Build Example

  • A - Magic: Full spellcasting ability is essential
  • B - Attributes: Need high mental stats for magic
  • C - Skills: Magic skills plus some mundane abilities
  • D - Metatype: Human or elf, the only choices at D
  • E - Resources: Magic doesn't require expensive gear

This creates a powerful mage who relies on spells over equipment.

Karma Costs

  • New Active Skill (Rating 1): 2 Karma
  • Improve Active Skill: New Rating × 2 Karma (Running from 2 to 3 costs 6)
  • Knowledge or Language Skill: New Rating × 1 Karma
  • Skill Group: New Rating × 5 Karma
  • Improve Attribute (including Edge): New Rating × 5 Karma
  • New Spell: 5 Karma
  • New Complex Form: 4 Karma
  • Specialization: 7 Karma
  • Initiation: 10 + (new grade × 3) Karma

Natural Progression

Your character grows organically based on what they do in-game:

  • Follow the story: Spend Karma on what your runner actually does; improvements take training time between runs
  • Contacts matter: NPCs can teach you new abilities
  • Gear upgrades: Better equipment as you earn more nuyen
  • Reputation: Success brings better job offers

Character Creation Checklist

  1. Concept First: What's your character's story and role?
  2. Priority Selection: A to E across the five columns, each letter once
  3. Metatype Choice: Human, elf, dwarf, ork or troll, as your Metatype priority allows
  4. Attribute Distribution: Only one Physical or Mental attribute may start at its natural maximum
  5. Magic/Resonance: Magician, mystic adept, adept, aspected magician, technomancer, or mundane
  6. Qualities: Up to 25 Karma of positive and 25 Karma of negative qualities
  7. Skill Selection: Plus free knowledge and language points equal to (Intuition + Logic) × 2
  8. Gear Purchase: Availability 12 or less and Rating 6 or less at creation
  9. Background Details: Contacts (free Karma equal to Charisma × 3), lifestyle, backstory
  10. Spend Your 25 Karma: Starting Karma buys qualities, spells, extra skills and more
  11. Final Calculations: Limits, Initiative and condition monitors

Common Newbie Mistakes

Avoid These Pitfalls

  • The "Do Everything" Character: Spreading points too thin makes you mediocre at everything
  • Ignoring Social Skills: Many problems can't be solved with bullets or code
  • Forgetting Basic Gear: Don't spend all your money on one amazing item
  • Stat Obsession: Remember that roleplay is more important than perfect numbers
  • Solo Mindset: Build for teamwork, not individual glory

Your Character Sheet as Dashboard

Think of your character sheet as the control panel for your shadowrunner. Every number tells a story about who your character is and what they can do. A high Firearms skill suggests a military background. Expensive cyberware implies corporate connections or successful runs. Low Resources but high Magic might mean a street shaman who awakened recently.

The beautiful thing about Shadowrun's system is that it encourages specialization while allowing flexibility. Your street samurai can learn some basic hacking. Your mage can pick up social skills. Your face can get combat training. The system grows with your character's story.
