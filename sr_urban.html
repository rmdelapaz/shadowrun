Urban Sprawl & Street Life

The Seattle Metroplex of 2075: its districts, the megacorps that run it, the SINless who live off the books, and what it costs to keep a roof over your head.

Welcome to the Megasprawl

The cities of 2075 aren't just bigger versions of today's urban centers - they're completely reimagined ecosystems where millions of people live, work, and die in vertical communities that stretch from underground warrens to sky-scraping arcologies. These aren't cities anymore; they're megasprawls - urban organisms that have consumed entire regions and developed their own climates, economies, and rules of survival.

Think of a megasprawl like a massive organism: the corporate towers are the brain, the industrial sectors are the muscles, the transportation networks are the circulatory system, and the streets are the nervous system where information, money, and violence flow in equal measure. Every shadowrunner needs to understand this urban ecosystem to survive, navigate, and profit within it.

The sprawl most Shadowrun games use is the Seattle Metroplex, and the people who really own it are the megacorporations. The ten largest, the AAA "Big Ten", sit on the Corporate Court at Zurich-Orbital, which grants them extraterritoriality: inside a AAA's facility, that corporation's law applies, not the city's. In 2075 the Court ranks them:

  1. Saeder-Krupp (owned outright by the great dragon Lofwyr)
  2. NeoNET
  3. Mitsuhama Computer Technologies
  4. Aztechnology
  5. Renraku
  6. Evo
  7. Ares Macrotechnology
  8. Shiawase
  9. Wuxing
  10. Horizon

Every one of them has offices, labs, or secrets somewhere in Seattle, which is why a Johnson is so often a corp in disguise.

The Vertical City: From Depths to Heights

Modern megasprawls are three-dimensional cities where your altitude determines your social status, security level, and life expectancy. Understanding the vertical geography is crucial for any urban operator:

The Social Elevator

Corporate Towers & Arcologies (the top floors)

The Ivory Towers: Executive suites, penthouse apartments, and exclusive corporate facilities. The air is literally cleaner here, filtered and climate-controlled. Security is absolute.

  • AAA corporate executives and board members
  • Dragon CEOs and their inner circles
  • Government officials with real power
  • Ultra-high net worth individuals

Mid-Level Corporate Housing (the middle floors)

The Hamster Wheels: Where corporate middle management lives in company-provided housing. Comfortable but controlled, with corporate surveillance masquerading as convenience.

  • Corporate middle management
  • Skilled technical professionals
  • Government bureaucrats
  • Successful small business owners

Street Level (ground and the first few floors)

The Concrete Reality: Where most of the population lives, works, and dies. This is the real city - chaotic, dangerous, and alive with possibility. Pollution, crime, and corporate neglect are facts of life.

  • Working class citizens
  • Small-time criminals
  • Shadow community members
  • Street vendors and hustlers

Underground (Basements, Sewers, Abandoned Infrastructure)

The Forgotten Realm: Beneath the official city lies another world entirely - homeless encampments, illegal clinics, hidden markets, and worse things that the surface world pretends don't exist.

  • SINless individuals
  • Escaped corporate experiments
  • Underground communities
  • Things that hunt in the dark

Your Place on the Ladder: SINs and Lifestyles

Most citizens carry a SIN (System Identification Number), the record that lets you hold a job, rent a flat, see a doctor, or ride the monorail. The SINless have none: they were born outside the system, lost their SIN, or had it erased. Most shadowrunners are SINless by choice or by accident and get by on fake SINs, which come in ratings 1 to 6 and cost Rating × 2,500¥. A cheap one fools a store scanner; only a good one survives a serious check.

In the rules, where you live is your Lifestyle, paid monthly:

  • Street: free. Sleeping rough, eating what you can find.
  • Squatter: 500¥. A corner of a condemned building or a coffin hotel.
  • Low: 2,000¥. A cramped apartment in a rough neighbourhood.
  • Middle: 5,000¥. A decent place, real food now and then, a wage-slave's life.
  • High: 10,000¥. A secure building in a good district.
  • Luxury: 100,000¥. The top of the tower.

Trolls pay 50% more for their Lifestyle, since everything has to be built bigger.

District Navigation: The Urban Ecosystem

Every megasprawl is divided into districts, each with its own character, dangers, and opportunities. Understanding these neighborhoods is essential for planning routes, finding contacts, and avoiding fatal mistakes.

The Seattle Metroplex has ten official districts: Auburn, Bellevue, Downtown, Everett, Fort Lewis, Puyallup, Redmond, Renton, Snohomish and Tacoma. Downtown is the corporate core. Redmond is so far gone that everyone calls it the Redmond Barrens; Touristville, the neighbourhood where outsiders go slumming, is part of it. Council Island is not a Metroplex district at all: it was ceded to the Salish-Shidhe Council. The cards below are the kinds of neighbourhood you will find across those districts. Gang Territories: The Real Law of the Streets

In the sprawl, corporate security only extends so far. Beyond their reach, the real power belongs to gangs - organized groups that control territory, run protection rackets, and provide their own brutal form of order:

Go-Gangers

The Road Warriors: Motorcycle and vehicle enthusiasts who control highways and smuggling routes.

  • Territory: Industrial corridors and highway interchanges
  • Business: Vehicle theft, smuggling, protection rackets
  • Threat Level: High mobility, vehicular combat specialists
  • Interaction: Useful for transportation and cargo moving

Halloweeners

The Chaos Bringers: Pyromaniacs and thrill-seekers who use fear and destruction as tools.

  • Territory: Abandoned districts and entertainment areas
  • Business: Arson, demolition, terror contracts
  • Threat Level: Unpredictable, explosive specialists
  • Interaction: Dangerous but effective for distraction jobs

The Ancients

The Elf-Pride Go-Gang: Elven motorcycle gang with a long memory and professional organization.

  • Territory: Elven communities and cultural districts
  • Business: Drug trafficking, weapons smuggling, bodyguard services
  • Threat Level: Well-organized, magically enhanced
  • Interaction: Professional if you show proper respect

Humanis Policlub

The Human Supremacists: Political organization with violent enforcement wings.

  • Territory: Human-majority neighborhoods
  • Business: Political violence, hate crimes, corporate backing
  • Threat Level: Corporate connections, political protection
  • Interaction: Extremely dangerous for metahumans

💡 Gang Interaction Tips

  • Respect Territory: Never enter gang turf without permission or protection
  • Know the Colors: Gang symbols and colors identify territory and allegiance
  • Pay the Toll: Moving through gang territory often requires payment or service
  • Neutral Ground: Some areas are gang-neutral by mutual agreement
  • Enemy of My Enemy: Gang conflicts create opportunities for shadowrunners

Security Ratings: Know Your Threat Level

Security providers rate every neighbourhood on a letter scale, from AAA at the top down through AA, A, B, C, D and E to Z, where nobody comes at all. The rating tells you how fast help arrives, how heavily armed it is, and how much anyone cares. Here are four points on that scale:

AAA Zone

Maximum Security

  • Corporate headquarters
  • Government facilities
  • Executive residential areas
  • Research installations

Response: Immediate, overwhelming force

AA Zone

High Security

  • Corporate subsidiaries
  • Upscale commercial areas
  • Middle management housing
  • Important infrastructure

Response: Professional security, quickly

A Zone

Standard Security

  • Commercial districts
  • Residential neighborhoods
  • Public transportation hubs
  • Entertainment venues

Response: Basic patrols, and they take their time

Z Zone

No Security

  • The Barrens
  • Abandoned districts
  • Underground areas
  • Gang territories

Response: You're on your own

Street Life: The Daily Grind

Life on the streets of 2075 is a constant struggle between survival and opportunity. Understanding the rhythms, rituals, and unwritten rules of street life is essential for any shadowrunner:

The Urban Ecosystem

Daily Survival Challenges

  • Food Security: Real food is expensive; most eat processed soy and krill
  • Clean Water: Municipal water is questionable; bottled water is a luxury
  • Air Quality: Pollution levels require masks or filters in many areas
  • Personal Safety: Violence is common; security is personal responsibility
  • Medical Care: Corporate healthcare for citizens; street docs for everyone else
  • Information Access: Matrix access varies wildly by location and social status

In-fiction street wisdom, not real numbers. "Corporate crime" means things that would be crimes if an individual did them; inside extraterritorial corporate property, they are simply policy.

Navigation Considerations

  • GridGuide System: Automated traffic control that tracks all registered vehicles
  • SIN Requirements: Most legal transport requires valid identification
  • Corporate Restrictions: Some routes are limited to corporate employees
  • Gang Tolls: Unofficial taxes for passage through gang territory
  • Time Factors: Rush hours create both cover and complications

Environmental Hazards: The Price of Progress

The sprawl isn't just dangerous because of people - decades of industrial pollution, magical contamination, and urban decay have created environmental threats that can kill the unprepared:

Air Pollution

Toxic smog requires filtration masks or respirators in many areas

Water Contamination

Industrial runoff and magical pollution make water sources dangerous

Toxic Zones

Areas of extreme contamination that cause immediate health effects

Magical Background

Negative magical environments that interfere with spellcasting

Structural Collapse

Aging infrastructure and poor maintenance create physical dangers

Radiation Zones

Legacy contamination from industrial accidents and wars

⚠️ Urban Survival Guide

  • Know Your Routes: Always have multiple paths to your destination
  • Respect the Locals: Gang territory, corporate zones, and tribal areas have rules
  • Carry Protection: Environmental and personal protection is essential
  • Watch the Weather: Acid rain and toxic storms are real threats
  • Trust Your Instincts: If an area feels wrong, it probably is
  • Have Backup Plans: Emergency routes and safe houses save lives

Digital Overlay: The Matrix in Physical Space

The physical and digital worlds aren't separate in 2075 - they're layered together through augmented reality, creating a hybrid environment where information and reality blend:

AR Integration in Urban Life

Augmented Reality Elements

  • Navigation Overlays: Real-time directions and route optimization
  • Identity Markers: AR tags showing people's public information
  • Commercial Advertising: Location-based marketing and promotion
  • Security Warnings: Threat assessments and danger zones
  • Social Networks: Digital communities overlaid on physical spaces
  • Gang Tags: Digital graffiti marking territory and allegiance

Digital Divide

Access to AR overlays depends heavily on your economic status and social position:

  • Corporate Citizens: Full AR integration with premium services
  • Middle Class: Standard AR with advertising and basic services
  • Working Poor: Limited AR access, heavy advertising
  • SINless: No legal account; they get by on cracked gear and borrowed or fake IDs

Encounter Protocols

Your Urban Survival Toolkit

Thriving in the megasprawl requires more than just combat skills and street smarts. You need to understand the complex web of relationships, territories, and unwritten rules that govern urban life. The city is a living organism, and successful shadowrunners learn to work with its rhythms rather than against them.

Remember: The street finds its own uses for things - including people. Stay flexible, stay informed, and always have an exit strategy. The sprawl rewards the adaptable and punishes the rigid.

Essential Urban Skills

  • Street Knowledge: Understanding local customs, gang territories, and safe routes
  • Social Navigation: Reading situations and adapting your approach
  • Resource Management: Making connections and trading favors
  • Threat Assessment: Recognizing danger before it finds you
  • Environmental Awareness: Understanding how the city's systems work
